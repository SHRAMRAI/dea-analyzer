How to read this map
Chute (F01–F82)
A numbered drop slot on the sorter belt. When the belt scans a package, it flips a paddle to push that package into the correct chute, where it falls into a cart below. This flat sorter has 82 chutes arranged in 4 columns.
Missort
A package that physically landed in the wrong chute. The sorter commanded it to one chute, but it ended up in a different one — so it gets loaded onto the wrong delivery van and goes to the wrong city.
System-recorded chute
Where the sorter thinks it sent the package. This is what the computer logged when it fired the divert paddle.
Physical destination (detection node)
Where the package actually showed up — detected by a scanner at a downstream delivery station or sort center that received the wrong package.
T1 — Physical overshoot
Package missed its chute by 1–3 belt positions. The diverter paddle fired at the right time but the package slid past — usually due to speed, weight, or paddle gap. Fix: diverter calibration.
T2 — Near-miss / routing lag
Package landed 4–15 positions away. Typically happens when a container closes/fills up mid-sort and the sorter keeps diverting to a nearby backup slot while the table updates. Fix: faster container turnover, less destination churn.
T3 — Full loop failure
Package traveled more than 15 belt positions past its intended chute — meaning it failed to divert on the left column, rode through the entire center-right and right sections, and eventually landed at F62 or another center-left chute. This is the most severe failure mode. Fix: allocation table propagation speed and re-read scanner sensitivity.
Stacking filter
The destination container label the package was sorted to — e.g. "DDX7-CART-SPG" means a cart going to DDX7 delivery station. Each chute holds one active container at a time.
Belt displacement (Δ positions)
How many chutes the package overshot or undershot from where it should have gone. A larger number means the error was more severe.
SmartPac / Jiffy Mailer
Lightweight flat packages (under 0.5 lbs). These are most prone to missorts because they're light enough to skip over the diverter paddle or keep sliding on the belt.
Received (physical) vs System-recorded
On the chute detail: red = packages that physically landed here (may include ones the system sent elsewhere). Blue = packages the system sent here (may have physically gone elsewhere). A big gap = that chute is a missort hotspot.
